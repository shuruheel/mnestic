## E2 source findings and tiny update control

At the measured candidate:

- `runtime/hnsw.rs::hnsw_search_level` bounds the retained nearest-neighbor heap
  by ef, not the visited set or vector cache. It traverses without an eligibility
  predicate. `hnsw_knn` evaluates the compiled filter on fetched candidate tuples
  only after the bottom-level search (lines 1257–1327). Increasing ef expands the
  global candidate pool; it does not make traversal eligibility-aware.
- `query/ra.rs::HnswSearchRA::iter` passes the query poison through its parent,
  then calls `hnsw_knn` synchronously. The inner HNSW loop receives no poison or
  estimated-memory budget, and `VectorCache::insert` has no budget charge.
  Outer checks remain cooperative; these loops do not establish a hard 250-ms
  or 64-MiB search bound.
- The Python `run_script` binding exposes timeout, but no governed snapshot handle
  or statement memory option. Five separately timed calls do not qualify the
  stable-snapshot widening gate. Native Rust governed transactions can retain a
  snapshot, but that alone does not establish accounting inside HNSW.
- FTS `candidates:` restricts before scoring/top-k and preserves corpus-global
  statistics. A normal join against global FTS top-k is not a substitute.

The [tiny update reproducer](0.19-filtered-retrieval/reproduce_updates.py) checks
rare-type keys beyond the initial global prefix, repeated calls, absent types,
metadata/vector upserts, equal-distance keys, deletion and rollback. It exits
nonzero when its full-coverage expectation fails; it is an investigation fixture,
not a passing CI test. Both released 0.18 and the candidate omitted key 24 after
an upsert in fresh builds at ef=50 (>25 rows). The candidate's SQLite build happened
to pass while its RocksDB build failed; the baseline failed on both. HNSW graph
randomness is not controlled. These observations establish a pre-existing quality
limitation, not its root cause or a candidate regression. Base-row loss is not
established by ANN omission. Preserve the [baseline](0.19-filtered-retrieval/baseline.log)
and [candidate](0.19-filtered-retrieval/candidate.log) failures.

Follow-up: the [mutation investigation](2026-11-05-hnsw-update-investigation.md)
now establishes graph disconnection as the cause in retained fixtures. Changing
a vector removes its old graph links; deletion does not reconnect neighbors.
Unfiltered ANN has the same omissions, ef=1600 does not recover them, and rebuilding
over identical rows restores coverage. This reproduces on both packages/backends.
The follow-up includes graph captures and a verified exact eligible-key example;
it does not repair the engine or establish a performance/routing threshold.
The [bounded-search draft](../specs/bounded-filtered-vector-search.md) separates
maintenance repair, resource accounting and experimental filtered traversal.

The original PR #65 fixture also ran on the optimized candidate: all 144
observations on both backends match the prior baseline pattern (rare keys absent
at ef=3/5, recovered at ef>=50), without ineligible or duplicate keys. These are
tiny correctness observations, not resource or latency qualification.

The filtered path therefore has not passed the deterministic tiny-fixture or
resource gate. No ANN redesign, new candidate API, layered backend, quantization
or plan cache is included. Exact eligible-key vector scans remain the quality
reference; their cost must be reported alongside the fixed-ef sweep.
